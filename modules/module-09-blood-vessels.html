Blood & Blood Vessels

What blood is made of, how it stops a leak, and why a tiny change in vessel width changes everything.

By the end of this page you should be able to
  • Name the parts of blood and the job of each
  • Explain how oxygen is carried, and why anemia causes shortness of breath
  • Walk through how bleeding is stopped, and find the positive feedback in it
  • Match each vessel type to what it is built to do
  • Explain why vessel width controls blood pressure and flow

1What is in the tube

PartShareJob
PlasmaAbout 55%Water, proteins, salts, nutrients, and wastes. The delivery fluid
Red cellsAbout 45%Carry oxygen, and help carry carbon dioxide back
White cellsUnder 1%Defense. See Module 11
PlateletsUnder 1%Plug damaged vessels and start clotting

Two plasma proteins do most of the work. Albumin holds water inside the vessels. Lose it and fluid leaks into the tissues as swelling. Fibrinogen is the raw material a clot is built from.

2Oxygen is carried, not just dissolved

Very little oxygen floats loose in plasma. Almost all of it rides on hemoglobin.

Each red cell is packed with hemoglobin, and each hemoglobin molecule carries up to four oxygen molecules.

Red cells have no nucleus. That frees up room for more hemoglobin, but it also means they cannot repair themselves. They last about 120 days and are replaced constantly.

Now the part that matters at the bedside. A patient with anemia can have normal lungs and a normal oxygen reading and still be starved of oxygen.

The reading shows how full the carriers are. It does not show how many carriers there are. Half the trucks, all fully loaded, still delivers half the cargo.

Why the nurse cares

Iron sits at the center of hemoglobin. No iron means no hemoglobin, which means no oxygen carried. That is why iron deficiency shows up as tiredness and shortness of breath rather than as anything that sounds like a blood problem.

3Stopping a leak, in three stages

  1. The vessel narrows. This happens at once, and it slows flow through the hole.
  2. Platelets plug it. They stick to the damaged wall, then release chemicals that call in more platelets, which release more chemicals. This is positive feedback, and it is what makes the plug form fast.
  3. The clot sets. A chain of clotting factors turns fibrinogen into fibrin, a mesh of threads that traps cells and turns the soft plug into a firm clot.

Positive feedback shows up here for the same reason it shows up in labor. The body wants the job finished quickly, and there is an outside event to stop it: the sealed vessel.

Compare that with the loops holding temperature and blood sugar steady, which are built never to finish at all.

Clotting factors are made in the liver, and many need vitamin K. That one fact explains why liver disease causes bleeding, and why warfarin, which blocks vitamin K, works as a blood thinner.

4Three vessel types, three different builds

VesselWallBuilt for
ArteryThick, stretchy, muscularHandling pressure, and springing back to keep flow going between beats
CapillaryOne cell thickExchange, and nothing else
VeinThin and floppy, with valvesHolding volume and returning blood at low pressure

Veins hold most of your blood at any moment, and they return it uphill against gravity with almost no pressure of their own.

They manage it two ways. Leg muscles squeeze them, and valves stop backflow. That is why standing still pools blood in the legs, and why moving prevents it.

5Width rules everything

Change a vessel's width a little and watch flow change a lot.

Vessel radius, resistance and flow

Flow through a tube depends on the fourth power of its radius. Cut the radius in half and flow drops to one sixteenth.

That is why arterioles, the small vessels wrapped in muscle, are the body's main control point for blood pressure. It is also why drugs that widen or narrow them have such big effects from such small changes.

Blood pressure itself is how much the heart pumps, multiplied by how much the vessels resist. Raise either one and pressure rises.

That single sentence is behind every blood pressure drug. They either reduce what the heart puts out, or they widen the vessels it pumps into.

Think of it this way

A garden hose with your thumb over the end. You barely moved your thumb, and the whole stream changed. Arterioles are the body's thumb.
