The Transport Bench

Every transport process is either falling down a gradient or spending energy to climb one. Here you change one Fick term at a time and watch flux respond, push a carrier past its transport maximum until glucose spills into urine, then inhibit the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase and watch every secondary active transporter weaken with it. Ask of each panel: what is the driving force, and who paid for it?
Clinical presets — each changes one term
Surface area is the term physiology exploits hardest. Microvilli on an enterocyte multiply A by roughly 20-fold, and folds plus villi plus microvilli bring the whole small intestine to about 200 square meters of absorptive surface.
Push a carrier system past its ceiling
The kidney filters glucose freely and then reabsorbs it on SGLT carriers. Carriers have a finite number of binding sites, so this route can saturate — and that is the cleanest experiment there is for telling a carrier from simple diffusion.
