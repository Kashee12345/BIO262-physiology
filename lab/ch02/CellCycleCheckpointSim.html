Checkpoints & Chemiosmosis

The cell cycle is a feedback-regulated control system, and cancer is what happens when the loop is cut. Set the conditions, run a full 24-hour cycle and watch each checkpoint decide whether the cell may proceed, arrest, repair or die. Then switch benches, take the oxygen away, and discover that dying properly costs ATP the cell may no longer have.
What kind of cell is this?
Phase lengths here are the real ones: G₁ 11 h, S 6–8 h for replication, G₂ 5 h, and mitosis itself only about one hour of a 24-hour cycle. Most of a cell’s life is interphase.
Who pays for everything
Compartmentation is a requirement, not tidiness. Lysosomal hydrolases need pH 4.8, which a cytosol at pH 7.2 would never tolerate; oxidative phosphorylation needs a proton gradient, and a gradient can only exist across a membrane tight enough to hold it. That membrane is the inner mitochondrial one.
