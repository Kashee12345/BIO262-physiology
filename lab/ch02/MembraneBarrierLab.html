The Bilayer Barrier

A lipid bilayer is an oily sheet about 3 nm thick between two salt solutions. That single fact decides what crosses, how fast, and — because a thin insulator between two conductors is a capacitor — how quickly the cell’s voltage can change. Throw solutes at the membrane below, buffer its fluidity with cholesterol, then switch modes and measure the membrane time constant τ = R × C for yourself.

The membrane as an electrical component
Specific capacitance sits near 1 microfarad per square centimeter in essentially every cell studied, because bilayer thickness is nearly universal. What differs between cells is the resistance — how many channels sit in parallel with that capacitor.

Why τ matters
Physiological membranes land in the 1–20 ms range. τ is the time the voltage needs to travel 63% of the way to its new value — and a large part of why action potentials rise and spread at the speed they do (Modules 5 and 6).
