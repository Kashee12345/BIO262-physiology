Reading the colors. Teal bases are exon; gray bases are intron. The gold-outlined bases are the intron’s GT and AG splice sites — the spliceosome finds the intron by them, so breaking one is a mutation in its own right even though it changes no codon.
What the nucleus does before anything is translated
TRANSCRIBE→5′ CAP→SPLICE→POLY-A→EXPORT
Only mature mRNA leaves through a nuclear pore. Separating transcription from translation in space and time is what lets the cell edit, censor and regulate the message before a single peptide bond is formed. The DNA itself never leaves.
