Salt, Sugar and Speed

One gland, two embryological origins, three jobs. The cortex defends volume with aldosterone and fuel with cortisol over hours; the medulla delivers a catecholamine surge in seconds — and cortisol is what makes that surge work at all. Use ΔP = Q × R as your guide: bleed the patient, restrict salt, block the renin system at four different points, and watch which term on the right actually moved.

The medulla is not a gland in the usual sense. Chromaffin cells are modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons that lost their axons: preganglionic fibers release acetylcholine onto them and they secrete straight into the blood. That is why the catecholamine effect reaches tissues with no sympathetic innervation and outlasts nerve traffic by a few minutes — and why cortical venous blood, extremely rich in cortisol, drains through the medulla and induces the enzyme that turns norepinephrine into epinephrine. The stress axis physically feeds itself.

Why cortisol has to be there

Permissive means one hormone must be present for another to work at full strength. Without cortisol, catecholamines cannot maintain vascular tone. That is precisely why an adrenal crisis presents as hypotension that responds poorly to fluids and to pressors until hydrocortisone is given. Drop the cortisol slider to zero, press the pressor button, and watch how little happens; then give hydrocortisone.
Read the pair, again. Low cortisol with a high ACTH means the adrenal cortex has failed — and because the zona glomerulosa fails too, expect hyponatremia, hyperkalemia and hyperpigmentation from the ACTH precursor peptide. Low cortisol with a low ACTH means the pituitary has failed or steroids were withdrawn; aldosterone, which is driven by angiotensin II and potassium rather than by ACTH, is largely preserved, so potassium is usually normal.
A note on the model. Baseline mean arterial pressure is taken as 93 mmHg and every hormone here is expressed as a multiple of its normal value, so you can see the direction and the relative size of each change. The baroreceptor reflex from Module 6 would blunt these swings within a few heartbeats — this panel deliberately shows the hormonal contribution on its own.
