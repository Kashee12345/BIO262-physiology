Two Set Points: Metabolic Rate and Ionized Calcium

One neck, two completely different control strategies. The thyroid stores months of hormone outside its cells, releases it slowly under pituitary command, and sets the pace of every mitochondrion in the body. The parathyroid keeps no store at all: its chief cell is both the sensor and the effector, reads ionized calcium through a surface receptor and answers within seconds. Break each one here and watch what the loop does about it.
Try a situation
Only the free fraction counts. Over 99 percent of circulating T4 travels bound to thyroxine-binding globulin. The bound pool is a buffer, not a signal — the pituitary reads only the free hormone. Raise the binding protein and watch the total climb while the free level and TSH refuse to move.
Try a situation
The set-point slider is the whole point of the loop. The chief cell reads ionized calcium through a surface calcium-sensing receptor. Move the set point and the cell defends a different number — that is what a parathyroid adenoma does, and it is why such a patient shows a high calcium with a PTH that looks deceptively normal.

T4 is mostly a prohormone. About 90 percent of what the gland secretes is T4. Peripheral deiodinases strip one iodine to make T3, which binds the nuclear receptor with roughly ten times the affinity. Take the iodine off the other ring instead and you get reverse T3, which is inactive — a regulated off-switch that rises in starvation and critical illness to lower metabolic demand. Drag the deiodinase slider down and watch TSH stay perfectly normal while T3 collapses.

Why the number has to be this tight. Extracellular calcium stabilizes voltage-gated sodium channels. Let it fall and the firing threshold drops, so nerves discharge on their own — perioral tingling, carpopedal spasm, laryngospasm. Let it rise and the threshold climbs, giving lethargy, weakness and constipation. Calcium is also the trigger for muscle contraction, exocytosis and clotting, so it is not allowed to drift.
Albumin changes the number without changing the physiology. Roughly 40 percent of plasma calcium is bound to albumin. Drop albumin from 4.0 to 2.0 g/dL and the total falls about 1.6 mg/dL — but the ionized calcium, the part the sensing receptor actually reads, has not moved and the patient has no symptoms. Correct for albumin, or measure the ionized level, before treating a number.
