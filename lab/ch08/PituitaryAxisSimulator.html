The Three-Tier Axis: Break a Tier, Read the Pair

Every axis in this module has the same architecture — hypothalamus, pituitary, gland — and the target hormone feeds back on both tiers above it. Damage one tier here and watch the loop hunt for a new steady state. Then read the tropic value and the target value together: that single habit localizes the lesion to the gland, the pituitary or the hypothalamus, and it is the most transferable idea in endocrine physiology.
Pick a lesion, or move the sliders yourself

Cut the stalk

The anterior pituitary has no direct innervation. Everything it does depends on releasing hormones delivered down the hypophyseal portal veins — two capillary beds in series, so a nanogram of releasing hormone arrives undiluted. Sever the stalk and every anterior hormone falls. Every one except prolactin.

Intact. TRH, CRH, GnRH and GHRH arrive down the portal veins; dopamine arrives too, and its job is to hold prolactin down.

Why GnRH must arrive in pulses

Amount is not the only variable — pattern is. GnRH must arrive in pulses roughly every 60–90 minutes to keep gonadotropes responsive. Give the same hormone continuously and the receptor downregulates and the axis shuts off, which is exactly how long-acting GnRH agonists are used.

The rule, in one line. Target low with tropic high means the gland failed. Both low means the pituitary or hypothalamus failed. Target high with tropic suppressed means something is producing hormone autonomously — a nodule, or a tablet. Read the pair, never the single value.
