Moving Air: Gradient, Compliance, Resistance

Nothing is ever sucked into a lung. Muscle enlarges the thorax, Boyle’s law drops alveolar pressure about 1 cmH2O below atmospheric, and Q = ΔP / R does the rest. Drive the breath below by changing effort, compliance and airway resistance, and watch alveolar, pleural and transpulmonary pressure either produce a 500 mL tidal volume or fail to. Then use the law of Laplace to find out why small alveoli do not empty into large ones.

Time constant τ = R × C. Normal is 2 × 0.2 = 0.4 s, so a 2-second inspiration is five time constants and the lung fills completely. Raise resistance or compliance far enough and the breath runs out of time before it runs out of gradient — that is air trapping, and it is why FRC rises in emphysema.

The breath, drawn

Read the middle trace. Alveolar pressure is negative only while air is flowing in and positive only while it is flowing out; it crosses zero exactly at end-inspiration and end-expiration, because at those instants there is no gradient and therefore no flow. The bottom trace never touches zero in a healthy chest — that persistent negative intrapleural pressure is what holds the lung open at a transpulmonary pressure of +5 cmH2O.

Two alveoli, one law: P = 2T / r

Surface tension at the air–liquid lining, not elastin, supplies about two-thirds of the lung’s recoil. For a sphere with one surface the collapsing pressure is P = 2T / r. Shrink the small alveolus and watch what the law predicts — then switch surfactant on.

Which way does the air go?

Why surfactant is not just a lubricant. Its molecules crowd together as the alveolus shrinks, so surface tension falls exactly when the radius falls. The ratio 2T/r stays roughly constant instead of exploding, small alveoli stay open, compliance rises and the work of breathing drops. Type II pneumocytes reach adequate output at about 34–36 weeks of gestation, which is the whole story of neonatal respiratory distress syndrome — and grunting against a partly closed glottis is a newborn generating its own positive end-expiratory pressure to fight this equation.
