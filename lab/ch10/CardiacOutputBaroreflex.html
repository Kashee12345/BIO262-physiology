Cardiac Output and the Loop That Defends It

Cardiac output has exactly two terms, CO = HR × SV, and stroke volume has exactly three determinants: preload, afterload and contractility. Set them below and the model recalculates the whole beat. Then disturb the circulation — stand up, bleed, squeeze a carotid sinus — and cut the sensor, the integrator or an effector to find out which part of the baroreceptor reflex was holding mean arterial pressure steady.

Frank–Starling: along the curve, or a new curve

Your contractility Normal reference Failing myocardium
Increasing preload moves the operating point rightward and upward along its curve. Increasing contractility lifts the whole curve, giving more stroke volume at any given preload. A failing ventricle sits on a flattened, depressed curve — which is why it responds so poorly to extra fluid and so well to reducing the load it faces.
