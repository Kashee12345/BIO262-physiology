Automaticity, Conduction and the ECG

Nodal cells have no stable resting potential: after repolarizing to about −60 mV they drift straight back up on the funny current until they hit threshold at −40 mV. Everything about heart rate is the slope of that drift. Change the autonomic tone, then break the pathway at the sinoatrial node, the AV node or a bundle branch, and read the ECG the damage produces.

The nodal upstroke is carried by calcium, not sodium, so it rises at 1–10 V/s against 200–500 V/s in a ventricular myocyte. That is why nodal tissue conducts at only 0.05 m/s.

Break the pathway

And independently
The AV node is the only electrical connection through the fibrous skeleton. Its 100 ms delay is functional, not incidental: it lets the atria finish emptying before the ventricles contract, and it filters very rapid atrial rates so the ventricles are protected when the atria fibrillate at 400–600 impulses per minute.

The path, and where you broke it

The gold ring marks whichever site is currently capturing the heart. The fastest drifter always wins, because it reaches threshold and depolarizes everything else before the slower sites finish — overdrive suppression. Slower pacemakers are only revealed when the faster one is removed or disconnected.
