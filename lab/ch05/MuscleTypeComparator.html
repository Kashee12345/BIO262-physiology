Smooth and Cardiac Muscle

All three muscle types slide actin past myosin. They differ in only three things: where the calcium comes from, which protein it binds, and whether the cells are electrically joined. Those three differences explain every functional contrast between them. Stimulate each tissue below and watch one tetanize, one refuse to, and one hold tension for hours almost for free.
Which tissue?
Which tissue?
Read the route, not the list. Skeletal muscle needs no calcium from outside the cell — the voltage sensor pulls the release channel open mechanically. Cardiac muscle needs a small trigger influx first. Smooth muscle draws on both and switches the myosin head rather than the actin filament. Everything in the table follows from that.
What is being held, and for how long?
Slow is a design choice, not a deficiency. Per cross-sectional area smooth muscle generates force comparable to skeletal muscle. It simply generates that force slowly — the myosin ATPase is roughly ten times slower — and then holds it at about one three-hundredth the ATP cost of an equivalent skeletal contraction.
