The Sarcomere: An Engine That Shortens by Sliding

Neither actin nor myosin ever changes length. Shortening is a geometric consequence of two rigid rods being pulled into greater overlap. Drag the sarcomere from 1.3 to 3.7 µm and watch the A band refuse to move while the I band and H zone narrow and vanish — then step a single myosin head through the four-part cycle and take its ATP away.

Show me
The geometry, fixed. The thick filament is 1.6 µm long and tethered at the M line. Each thin filament is 1.0 µm long and anchored to a Z disc. Everything on the readout to the right is arithmetic on those two numbers plus the length you choose. A band = 1.6 µm. I band = length − 1.6. H zone = length − 2.0.
Drive one myosin head
Watch the accounting. One ATP is consumed per head per cycle. It is hydrolyzed in step 1 to cock the head, its energy is spent in step 3, and a fresh ATP must bind in step 4 simply to let go — that binding is not a hydrolysis.
