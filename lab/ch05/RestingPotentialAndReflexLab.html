Graded potentials decay with distance. Current leaks out through the membrane as it spreads, so a 10 mV potential may be only 4 mV a fraction of a millimeter away. The distance over which it falls to 37 percent of its starting value is the length constant, typically 0.1 to 1 mm. That is why graded potentials cannot carry information far — and why they are still the currency of integration.
Break a component of the loop
Name every element as you watch it. Regulated variable: muscle length. Sensor: the muscle spindle. Afferent: a large Ia fiber, among the fastest in the body at 80 to 120 m/s. Integrator: the spinal cord, through a monosynaptic Ia-to-alpha-motor-neuron connection, which is why the whole loop takes only 20 to 40 ms. Effector: the extrafusal muscle. Response: contraction.
