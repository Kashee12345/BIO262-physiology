From Nerve to Calcium

A healthy junction produces an end-plate potential of about 70 mV when only about 35 mV is needed to carry the membrane from −90 to −55 mV. That roughly twofold margin is the safety factor. Shrink it here — at the receptor, at the presynaptic calcium channel, at the SNARE, at the esterase — then run a stimulation train and read the pattern. Then fire the impulse down a T tubule and watch cytosolic calcium rise a hundredfold.

Repetitive nerve stimulation
The numbers behind the model. One nerve impulse releases about 100 to 200 vesicles, each carrying 5,000 to 10,000 acetylcholine molecules, across a cleft only 20 to 50 nm wide. Release depends on the fourth power of calcium entry, which is why a modest loss of presynaptic calcium channels is devastating. Acetylcholinesterase clears the cleft in about 1 ms.
