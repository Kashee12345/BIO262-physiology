The Action Potential: Threshold, Refractoriness and Speed

A graded potential is proportional to its stimulus and dies out. An action potential is not, and does not. Drive a stimulus up through the −55 mV threshold and watch a proportional response turn abruptly into an identical, self-regenerating spike — then fire a second stimulus into the wake of the first to find the refractory periods, and strip myelin off an axon to watch conduction slow and then fail outright.

Second stimulus — test the refractory periods
The experiment that proves all-or-none. Set the first stimulus to 15 mV and pin the trace. Now set it to 30 mV and pin again. Then 45 mV. Three pinned traces, three very different stimuli, one waveform. Amplitude carries no information about stimulus size — which is exactly why the nervous system has to code intensity as frequency and recruitment instead.

Two things to try. Take a 10 µm myelinated fiber down to about 35% myelin and it still conducts, slowly. Now raise the temperature to 39 °C and it stops. Nothing was added to the lesion — heat simply shortens the time voltage-gated Na+ channels stay open, and a safety factor that was already marginal falls below one.
