The Brain as a Control System: Perfusion and Set Points

The brain is a controller that has to be perfused inside a rigid box, and a box whose contents are fixed at about 1,400 mL of brain, 150 mL of blood and 150 mL of CSF. Grow something inside it and watch CSF give way, then blood, then pressure. Then move down to the hypothalamus and run its thermostat — including the version where pyrogens move the set point and the patient shivers at 39 °C because the body is defending its new target.

The loop, labeled. Regulated variable: core body temperature. Sensors: peripheral thermoreceptors in skin and central thermosensitive neurons in the preoptic area. Integrator: the hypothalamus, comparing measured temperature against the set point. Effectors: cutaneous arterioles, sweat glands, skeletal muscle and the thyroid axis. Watch each one move as you change the environment.
