The Baroreceptor Reflex: Break the Loop and Watch

Stand up and 500–800 mL of blood pools in your legs within seconds. Venous return falls, stroke volume falls, arterial pressure falls — and you do not faint, because the medulla notices and answers within a few heartbeats. Watch the order of events. Vagal tone is withdrawn first, because the vagus acts through M2 receptors coupled to Gi and potassium channels — the effect appears within one or two beats. Sympathetic tone through β1 and α1 takes several seconds, because those are G protein–coupled second messenger cascades. The brake is released before the accelerator is pressed, and the same order explains why the first 30–40 bpm of the heart rate rise at the start of exercise come from vagal withdrawal rather than from sympathetic activation.
Why the heart is slower than its own pacemaker. The intrinsic firing rate of the sinoatrial node is 100–110 beats per minute, yet resting heart rate is about 70. The difference is continuous vagal restraint. Choose atropine and watch the restraint disappear.
