The Synapse: Summation at the Axon Hillock

A single EPSP is worth about 0.5–1 mV, and the neuron must climb 15 mV from −70 to −55 to fire. So no synapse ever decides anything alone. Drive excitatory and inhibitory inputs into a cell and watch thousands of graded, analog votes get summed in the dendrites and converted, at one point, into a digital output — then move to the terminal and take away the calcium or the receptors and watch a perfectly healthy axon fail to say anything at all.

What the bars mean. Repetitive stimulation depletes the readily releasable vesicle pool, so the fifth response is normally about a quarter smaller than the first. A healthy junction never notices, because it releases three to four times more transmitter than it needs. Take that margin away and the decrement becomes visible — which is precisely the electrodiagnostic sign of myasthenia gravis.
