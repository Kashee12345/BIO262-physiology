Fever: A Loop Defending a Moved Set Point

Core temperature normally sits near 37 °C. In fever nothing about the control loop is broken — prostaglandin E2 simply raises the target, and the same negative feedback then defends the new one. Drive pyrogen into the loop below, block the cascade wherever you like, and watch the chill phase, the plateau and the crisis fall out of the arithmetic. Then switch the environment to heat stroke and see why the same antipyretic does nothing.

The stimulus and where you block it

The chain is LPS → macrophage → IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α → vascular organ of the lamina terminalis → PGE2 → raised set point. A COX inhibitor cuts the last enzymatic step; a cytokine blocker cuts an earlier one. Both lower the set point, and neither cools a body whose set point was never raised.

Twenty-four hours of core temperature

Above roughly 40–41 °C the cost of protein denaturation and seizures outweighs the benefit of fever, which is otherwise useful: it accelerates leukocyte and enzyme activity, sequesters the plasma iron and zinc bacteria need, and impairs replication of some pathogens.
