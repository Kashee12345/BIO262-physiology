Two Signals, Then the Kill

A T cell that recognizes antigen does nothing unless a second, danger-dependent signal says the antigen matters. Set the three signals below and watch the logic gate resolve into activation or anergy. Then run a viral infection forward: cytotoxic T cells read MHC class I, natural killer cells read its absence, and turning that one dial down opens an escape window where neither can finish the job.

The battlefield

Normal CD4 count is 500 to 1,500 cells per µL. Below 200 the patient meets the definition of AIDS and cell-mediated immunity fails first, because most B-cell class switching also needs helper input.

Scenarios

The tissue, live

Twenty-four days of the infection

The cytotoxic T cell forms an immunological synapse and releases perforin and granzymes into a narrow cleft, so neighboring healthy cells are spared. The target dies by apoptosis, not lysis: it shrinks, fragments its DNA including any viral genome, and is packaged into membrane-bound bodies for phagocytosis. Nothing infectious spills out. The killer then detaches, reloads and kills again.
