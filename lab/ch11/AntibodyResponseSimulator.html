Building an Antibody Response

A first exposure lags 5 to 10 days, peaks modestly and is IgM-dominated. A second begins in 1 to 3 days, peaks roughly 100 to 1,000 times higher and is high-affinity IgG. Nothing new is invented in between — there are simply more precursors, already class-switched and affinity-matured. Place exposures on the timeline below and build that difference yourself.

Choose what you are giving

Age only matters for one antigen class. Pure polysaccharide antigens cannot be presented on MHC, so they are T-independent: they cross-link B-cell receptors directly, produce mostly IgM, generate little class switching and poor memory — and they work badly under 2 years. Try the same sugar as a conjugate.

Inside the germinal center

A naive B cell binds native antigen, internalizes it and presents the peptides on MHC class II. A follicular helper T cell specific for the same antigen recognizes that display and delivers CD40 ligand and cytokines — linked recognition, a safety interlock ensuring the B cell is reacting to an antigen the T cell already vetted. Then class switch recombination swaps the heavy-chain constant region (interferon-gamma favors IgG, TGF-β favors IgA, IL-4 favors IgE) while somatic hypermutation mutates the variable region about a million times faster than background, and the best binders win the limited antigen on follicular dendritic cells. A surviving plasma cell secretes up to about 2,000 antibody molecules per second.
