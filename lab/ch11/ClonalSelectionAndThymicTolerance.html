Clonal Selection and Thymic Tolerance

Your lymphocytes are already specific for antigens you have never met, including molecules that do not exist in nature, because random recombination of V, D and J segments builds roughly 1011 receptor specificities before antigen arrives. Tolerance therefore has to be built rather than inherited. Move the two thymic thresholds and watch that curation happen, then release an antigen into the surviving repertoire and watch one clone — and only one — expand.

Two tests, in order, on every thymocyte

Positive selection (cortex) asks only whether the receptor can engage self-MHC at all — a receptor that cannot is useless, because every antigen it will ever see is displayed on self-MHC, so those cells die by neglect. Negative selection (medulla) asks the opposite: does it bind self peptide too strongly? AIRE makes medullary epithelium express thousands of tissue-restricted proteins — insulin, thyroglobulin, myelin — so a thymocyte tours the whole body without leaving the thymus. Normally only about 2 percent pass both tests.

Antigen selects; it does not instruct

A single selected clone expands roughly 1,000-fold in about a week — a doubling every 17 hours or so. Nothing about the receptor changes; the antigen simply acts as a selective pressure on a population that already existed. That is why a first infection has a several-day symptomatic window before adaptive control arrives.
