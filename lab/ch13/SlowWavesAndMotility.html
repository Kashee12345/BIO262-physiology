Slow Waves, Spikes and the Motor Patterns of the Gut

Interstitial cells of Cajal oscillate the smooth-muscle membrane between roughly −60 and −40 mV, but a slow wave on its own moves nothing. Force appears only when a wave peak is pushed past threshold and spike potentials fire on top of it. Shift the baseline with excitatory and inhibitory input below, count the spikes, and see the same fixed rhythm produce anything from silence to a full propulsive sweep.
