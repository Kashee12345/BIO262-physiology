The Calcium Loop

Plasma ionized calcium is held near 1.2 mmol/L by a sensor that works backwards: calcium binding to the CaSR suppresses PTH, so a fall releases the brake. Knock calcium down and watch the kidney answer in minutes, bone in hours and the gut in a day or two. Then break the sensor, the gland or an effector, and read the pattern you get.
What happens at hour 6?
Break something in the loop
Name the loop as you watch it. Variable: plasma ionized calcium. Sensor: the calcium-sensing receptor on the parathyroid chief cell, set point near 1.2 mmol/L. Integrator: the parathyroid gland. Effectors: distal renal tubule, bone, and intestine via calcitriol. Response: a rise in ionized calcium that re-occupies the CaSR and shuts the signal off.
A calcium panel to interpret
corrected Ca = measured Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 − albumin) and ionized calcium falls about 0.05 mmol/L for every 0.1 unit rise in pH
Total calcium is a bookkeeping convenience. Roughly 45% is bound to protein, chiefly albumin, about 10% is complexed to citrate, phosphate and bicarbonate, and only 45–50% circulates free. The homeostatic system does not know or care what the total is.
A typical adult day, in milligrams
The kidneys filter on the order of 10,000 mg per day of the non-protein-bound fraction. Small changes in the reabsorbed fraction therefore move very large amounts of calcium — which is why the renal arm is the fast one.
