Levers, Cartilage and the Strain Ladder

Three mechanical stories share one skeleton. Muscles work through third-class levers and must therefore pull many times harder than the loads they lift. Joints slide on water pressurized inside cartilage, not on a slippery coating. And a fracture heals up a ladder of tissues chosen by one physical variable, interfragmentary strain, which the healing tissue then lowers for itself.
Which joint are we loading?
muscle force = load × load arm ÷ effort arm torque equals force times the perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation
How is the joint being used?
Articular cartilage is 65–80 percent water by weight, held in a type II collagen network by the giant proteoglycan aggrecan. Aggrecan’s densely packed sulfate and carboxyl groups draw counter-ions in, water follows osmotically, and the tissue sits permanently swollen and pre-tensioned against its own collagen restraint.
