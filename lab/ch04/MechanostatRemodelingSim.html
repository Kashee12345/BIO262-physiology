The Mechanostat and the RANKL Switch

Bone does not regulate its own mass — it regulates strain, and mass is merely how it hits the target. Load a skeleton and watch strain walk back into the window as bone is added. Then work the switch every hormone and drug ultimately uses, the RANKL/OPG ratio, and finally dig and refill basic multicellular units to see why a three-week hole takes four months to fill.
Loading history to simulate
The point of the loop. Strain, not mass, is the controlled variable. Add bone and the same load produces less strain, so the anabolic signal switches itself off. Take load away and strain falls below the disuse threshold, so the system removes bone until strain rises again.
Send a signal to the osteoblast lineage
Osteoclasts cannot be commanded directly by most hormones — they lack the receptors. The bone-forming lineage tells the bone-destroying lineage what to do, and it does so by setting the ratio of a ligand to its own decoy.
Basic multicellular units
Roughly 10 percent of the adult skeleton is replaced every year by BMUs, with 1–2 million active at any moment and a new one starting somewhere about every 10 seconds. Each digs for 2–3 weeks, pauses 1–2 weeks, then refills over 3–4 months.
