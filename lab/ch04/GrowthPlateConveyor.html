The Growth Plate Conveyor

Mineralized matrix cannot expand from within, so a bone lengthens in exactly one place: the epiphyseal plate, where cartilage is manufactured at the top and replaced by bone at the bottom. Drive the conveyor with growth hormone, thyroid hormone, glucocorticoid and estrogen; then run a whole childhood and find out which hormone closes the plate. Finally, widen a shaft by modeling and see why a thinner bone can stay just as hard to bend.
Hormonal drive on the plate
Try a case
Watch the plate thickness. Cartilage is added at the top at the same rate bone replaces it at the bottom, so the plate does not thin as growth proceeds. What moves is the epiphysis, carried steadily further from the mid-shaft.
Whose childhood are we running?
Set estrogen to 0% and the plate never closes — that is aromatase deficiency or a defective estrogen receptor. Then press the button below to give estrogen at a chosen age and watch the plates fuse.
Modeling: two surfaces, two independent cell teams
Modeling is osteoblasts and osteoclasts acting on different surfaces to change a bone’s shape. The periosteum adds bone to the outside while the endosteum removes it from the inside, so the shaft widens and the marrow cavity enlarges. Contrast remodeling, where the two are coupled on the same surface and the purpose is renewal, not reshaping.
