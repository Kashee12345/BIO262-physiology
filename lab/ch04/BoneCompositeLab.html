The Bone Composite Bench

Bone is an interpenetrating composite: hydroxyapatite crystals grown inside collagen fibrils, where mineral carries compression and collagen carries tension. Damage one phase at a time and watch the stress–strain curve change shape — brittle, soft, or simply too little bone. Then switch to the osteoclast bench and take away its proton pump or its cathepsin K.
Clinical presets
Matrix by wet weight is about 65% mineral, 25% organic, 10% water; roughly 90% of the organic fraction is type I collagen. The third slider changes how much bone there is without changing what it is made of.
How is it being loaded?
The sealed resorption lacuna
A resorbing osteoclast seals a patch of bone with an integrin-rich actin ring and behaves like a miniature stomach turned upside down. Two chemically separate jobs have to happen in order: dissolve the mineral with acid, then digest the exposed collagen with a protease. Switch pieces of the machinery off and see which job stops.
