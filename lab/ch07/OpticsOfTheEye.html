The Sixty-Diopter Eye

The relaxed eye totals about 60 diopters, and roughly two-thirds of that comes from the cornea, not the lens. Move the axial length by a single millimeter below and watch the focal point walk off the retina; flood the eye with water and watch the cornea lose almost all its power. Then age the lens, and finally clog the trabecular meshwork and read intraocular pressure straight off Q = ΔP / R.

Build the eye
The one-millimeter experiment. Leave every other control alone and move axial length from 24.0 to 25.0 mm. The error jumps by about 2.6 D — a single millimeter of eyeball is worth a real spectacle prescription. Nothing is wrong with the retina in any of these states; the image simply arrives at the wrong plane.

Where is the target?
The paradox to watch. The ciliary muscle contracts to make the lens more powerful. It is a ring, so contraction narrows its diameter like a purse-string, the zonular fibers go slack, and the elastic capsule lets the lens round up. Slide the effort control and watch the zonules loosen as the muscle tightens.

The hydraulics
Why pressure rises. Production is almost pressure-independent — the ciliary epithelium secretes actively using Na+/K+-ATPase and carbonic anhydrase, and it does not slow down just because the eye is tense. So when resistance climbs, the only variable left free to move is pressure.
