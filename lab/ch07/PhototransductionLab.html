The Dark Current

Photoreceptors break the rule that stimuli depolarize. In darkness cGMP holds channels open, Na+ and Ca2+ pour in, and the rod sits at about −40 mV releasing glutamate continuously — darkness is the maximum signal. Turn the light up below and watch cGMP fall, channels shut, and the cell hyperpolarize toward −70 mV. Then dark-adapt a retina over thirty minutes, and build a center-surround receptive field out of lateral inhibition.
How much light is falling on this rod?
Fire a brief flash and watch it recover
The claim to test. Set illumination to darkness and read the membrane potential: about −40 mV, unusually depolarized for a neuron, with glutamate release at maximum. Now step the light up. Everything falls — cGMP, open channels, dark current, membrane potential and glutamate. Any description of “more glutamate from a rod” is a description of darkness.
Step from bright light into a dark room
Watch for the break. Sensitivity improves in two limbs, not one. Cones finish in about 5–7 minutes and then stop improving; the curve bends at the rod–cone break and rods carry on for another 20–30 minutes, ending roughly 100,000 times more sensitive than where they started.

Shine light on the receptive field
The result that surprises people. Diffuse light over the whole receptive field — the brightest stimulus available — barely changes the firing rate at all. Center and surround cancel. The retina is not built to report how much light there is. It is built to report where the light changes.
