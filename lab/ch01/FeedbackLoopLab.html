Feedback Loop Lab

Every homeostatic loop has the same five parts: a regulated variable, a sensor, an integrator, an effector, and a response. Push the variable off its set point with a disturbance, then break one component at a time and watch the variable run away. Change the loop gain and see the rule that proportional feedback corrects most of an error but never all of it.

Positive feedback is not a mistake

Amplifying loops exist wherever the body wants an endpoint, not a steady state. In labor, head pressure on the cervix drives oxytocin, which drives stronger contractions — and stops at delivery. In hemostasis, activated platelets recruit more platelets and thrombin catalyzes its own production — and stops when the plug seals the vessel. In the action potential upstroke, sodium entry opens more sodium channels — and stops when those channels inactivate within about a millisecond. Every physiologic positive loop is short, explosive, and self-terminating. When the brake fails, you get disseminated intravascular coagulation or the downward spiral of cardiogenic shock.
