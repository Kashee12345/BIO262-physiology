Acid–Base Lab

Arterial pH is not set by bicarbonate and it is not set by carbon dioxide. It is set by the ratio of the two: pH = 6.1 + log([HCO3−] ÷ 0.03 × PaCO2). Why so tiny a concentration matters

At pH 7.4 the hydrogen ion concentration is 40 nmol/L — roughly three million times lower than the 140 mmol/L of sodium. The body is not fighting a large mass of acid; it is defending an extraordinarily dilute species. Hydrogen ions bind avidly to the negatively charged side chains of proteins, so a shift of 0.2 pH units changes the shape of thousands of enzymes, channel gates and binding pockets at once. Acidemia depresses myocardial contractility and blunts the vascular response to catecholamines; alkalemia drives ionized calcium onto albumin, which is why a hyperventilating patient gets perioral tingling and carpopedal spasm. Values below 6.8 or above 7.8 are not survivable for long.
