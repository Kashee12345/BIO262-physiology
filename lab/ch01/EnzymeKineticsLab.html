Enzyme and ATP Lab

A protein works only in the shape it folds into, and that shape is held by weak bonds that heat and pH extremes can undo. Choose a protein, then drag substrate to find where it saturates, drag temperature past 41 °C to denature it, and move pH away from its home compartment. The last two settings show what saturation costs a patient and what a gradient costs a cell.

1 · Choose the protein

1.0 × Km
Rate rises with substrate until every active site is occupied. After that the enzyme is saturated and adding more substrate changes nothing.
37.0 °C
Human enzymes are optimized near 37 °C and begin to lose activity above about 41 to 42 °C. That is the molecular reason a core temperature above 40 °C is an emergency.
7.4
Each protein is folded correctly only in its own compartment. Pepsin needs pH 2; trypsin needs pH 8; move either into the other's home and it stops working.
