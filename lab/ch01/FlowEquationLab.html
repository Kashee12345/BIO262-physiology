The Flow Equation Lab

Everything that moves in the body moves down a gradient at a rate set by the opposition it meets: Q = ΔP / R. Resistance follows Poiseuille's relationship, so it rises with viscosity and length but falls with the fourth power of the radius. Pick a system, then drag the radius and watch what a small change in a tube does to the flow through it.

Why the fourth power matters

Halve the radius and resistance rises sixteen-fold (24), so at a fixed driving pressure flow falls to one-sixteenth. That is why arterioles, not arteries, control where blood goes, and why a bronchiole narrowed by half in an asthma attack collapses airflow — the driving pressure for quiet breathing is only 1 to 2 mm Hg, so there is nothing spare to overcome it. The same skeleton describes solutes crossing a membrane (gradient = concentration difference, resistance = membrane permeability), heat moving down a temperature gradient, and water moving down osmotic and hydrostatic pressure gradients.
