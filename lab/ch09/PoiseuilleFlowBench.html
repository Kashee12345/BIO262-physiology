Q = ΔP / R: The Fourth-Power Bench

Resistance is R = 8ηL / πr4. Viscosity and length are fixed in the short term; radius is not, and it is raised to the fourth power. Halve a vessel’s radius and resistance rises sixteen-fold. Move the radius slider a few percent and watch flow, the pressure profile down the whole tree, capillary pressure and the arterial pulse all rearrange themselves.

Where the resistance lives

Mean pressure falls only about 3 mmHg from the aorta to the small arteries, then plunges roughly 55 mmHg across the arterioles. The largest pressure drop marks the largest resistance — which is why a vessel 10 to 100 µm across, with a disproportionately thick ring of smooth muscle, is the control point of the entire circulation.
