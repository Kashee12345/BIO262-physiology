The Hematocrit Trade-off

Packing more red cells into every milliliter raises the oxygen each milliliter carries — but it also thickens the blood, and viscosity sits in the numerator of the resistance equation. Drag the hematocrit below and watch the two effects fight. Total oxygen delivery is the product of content and flow, and it peaks at a hematocrit very close to the one you actually have.

Why the tube alone can fool you

Hematocrit is a ratio, not an amount. Acute hemorrhage removes cells and plasma in the same proportion, so the packed column and the plasma column shrink together and the reading barely moves for the first hour or two. It falls only once interstitial fluid shifts in and dilutes what is left. Try the Acute hemorrhage, 1 hour chip: the tube looks normal while a liter is missing.
Resting oxygen consumption is about 250 mL/min. Watch the delivery figure against that number — the four-fold margin at rest is what exercise, anemia and hypoxemia all spend.
