Why the square mattersDiffusion is not slow over short distances — a small solute crosses 1 µm of cytosol in about 0.5 ms. But because time scales with distance squared, 1 mm takes on the order of 500 seconds and 10 mm takes close to 14 hours. That is why no cell sits more than about 100–200 µm from a capillary, why alveolar walls are only 0.2–0.6 µm thick, and why anything larger than a millimeter needs a circulation rather than patience.
Misconception. Tight junctions do not hold cells together. They seal the paracellular pathway; desmosomes and hemidesmosomes bear the mechanical load. If tight junctions were the glue, blistering diseases would target claudin — they target cadherins instead.
