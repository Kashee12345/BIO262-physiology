Editing the Filtrate: Tm, Threshold and Clearance

Every reabsorptive step in the nephron is bought with the sodium gradient set by the basolateral Na+/K+ ATPase, and every carrier eventually saturates. Push plasma glucose upward against a transport maximum of about 375 mg/min and generate the titration curve yourself; watch sugar appear in the urine at 180–200 mg/dL, well before the whole-kidney Tm is reached. Then walk sodium down the segments and measure a clearance.

Load a case
Filtered load = GFR × plasma concentration. At 125 mL/min and 100 mg/dL that is 125 mg/min — a third of what the carriers can handle, so the urine is glucose free. Raise either term and you climb the same curve.

U and P only need to share units — they cancel. Clearance is the volume of plasma completely cleared of a substance each minute, which is why it can exceed renal plasma flow only if you have made an arithmetic error, and why it equals renal plasma flow for a substance that is both filtered and almost entirely secreted.
