Pressure Natriuresis: the Kidney Sets Blood Pressure

Baroreceptors correct pressure in seconds and then adapt. The kidney corrects it over days and never adapts: as long as arterial pressure sits above set point it keeps excreting sodium and water, so volume falls until pressure comes back. That is what “infinite gain” means. Set sodium intake, shift the renal function curve with RAAS, ANP or lost nephron mass, then press Run the days and watch pressure settle wherever excretion finally equals intake.

What the curve means. Each point answers one question: at this arterial pressure, how much sodium does the kidney put out in a day? Where the curve crosses the intake line, output equals input and volume stops changing — and that crossing, not the heart and not the arterioles, is where mean arterial pressure comes to rest. Notice how steep the normal curve is: that steepness is why a healthy person can eat wildly different amounts of salt and barely move their pressure.

Forty days of chasing balance

Sodium excretion (gold) climbs or falls toward the intake line while pressure (petrol) drifts to whatever value makes that happen. Nothing here is a set point being read by a sensor — pressure simply stops moving when the two flows match, and it will not stop before then.
