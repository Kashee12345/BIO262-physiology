One Axis, Two Patterns: the Surge and the Steady State

Both sexes run the same three tiers: pulsatile GnRH, two pituitary gonadotropins, gonadal steroids feeding back. The female cycle differs from the male steady state because of a single inversion — when estradiol passes about 200 pg/mL and stays there for roughly 48 hours, its effect on the hypothalamic–pituitary unit flips from inhibitory to stimulatory. Scrub through the days below, break the inversion, and see what a cycle looks like without it. Then switch to the male axis and suppress it with exogenous androgen.
Change the axis
The luteal phase does not move. The corpus luteum lives 12 to 14 days and then involutes unless hCG rescues it, so lengthening the cycle lengthens the follicular phase only. Slide cycle length from 21 to 35 and watch ovulation walk to the right while the distance from ovulation to the next menses stays put — that is why you count back 14 days to find the ovulation day.
Change the axis
Pulsatility is the message. GnRH must arrive in pulses every 60 to 120 minutes. Deliver it continuously and pituitary receptors downregulate and the axis switches off — not a side effect but the therapeutic mechanism of GnRH agonists.
