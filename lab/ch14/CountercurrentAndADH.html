The Medullary Gradient and the ADH Loop

The loop of Henle multiplies a 200 mOsm/kg single effect into a corticomedullary gradient running from 300 to about 1200 mOsm/kg; the vasa recta preserve it passively and urea tops it up. ADH then decides how much of that gradient the collecting duct is allowed to use. Move plasma osmolality and blood volume below, break NKCC2 or the urea supply, and watch the tubular fluid profile, urine osmolality and daily urine volume follow.

Read the two halves separately. The gradient sliders build the opportunity to concentrate; osmolality and volume decide whether ADH takes it. A patient can fail to concentrate because the hormone is missing, because the receptor cannot answer it, or because the medulla has no gradient left to use — three different lesions with the same dilute urine.
